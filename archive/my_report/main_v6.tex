\section{Overview}

This week I finished the analysis pipeline for the first subject
(SK1, my own recording).
The idea was to get everything working on one subject first before
moving on to the rest.
I computed four main metrics across all 11 channels -- alpha SNR, alpha
reactivity (the Berger effect~\cite{kirschfeld2005}), correlation with the disc electrode, and VEP
amplitude.
I also ran FOOOF~\cite{donoghue2020} to separate oscillatory peaks from the 1/f background,
and used SSIM~\cite{wang2004} to compare spectrograms between each tEEG/eEEG pair,
which was based on your suggestion to try image processing on the spectrograms.

The main takeaway is that the paste TCRE~\cite{besio2006} (Ch9/Ch10) looks the best
across all the metrics.
The felt tEEG channels (Ch1, 3, 5, 7) have a lot of ADC clipping,
which like you pointed out is probably from high impedance.
That needs to be fixed before recording more subjects.
I put the individual PSD plots and per-channel spectrograms in the
appendix so the main report is not too long.

\section{Signal Quality: The Main Issue}

Five channels are clipping at the ADC limits ($\pm$3,276.7\,$\mu$V):
Ch1, Ch3, Ch5, Ch7, and Ch11.
Like you mentioned, this is most likely high impedance causing the amplifier
to pick up a lot of movement artifact.
The paste TCRE and the eEEG channels stayed within range, which is
why they look much cleaner.

\textbf{Action item:} measure impedances at the start of every session.
I will also try applying more conductive gel to the felt electrodes before
the next subject.

\section{Alpha Reactivity: Why It Is All Below 1}

Alpha reactivity is the ratio of eyes-closed to eyes-open alpha power.
If it is above 1, that means alpha increases when you close your eyes --
that is the Berger effect~\cite{kirschfeld2005}, and that is what we expect to see.
Right now every channel is below 1, meaning open-eye alpha is actually
higher, which is the opposite of what we want.

I think this is because the visual stimulation blocks (S7 events)
happened during the open-eye periods, and on the channels that are clipping
those blocks probably pushed up the broadband power a lot.
So I think it is more of an artifact issue than anything real.
Ch3 came closest at 0.81$\times$, and Ch9 was the best non-clipping
channel at 0.64$\times$.
I am hoping once we fix the impedance problem this will look more
like what we expect.

\section{FOOOF Spectral Parameterization}

I used the FOOOF (specparam) algorithm~\cite{donoghue2020} on each channel to try to
separate the oscillatory alpha peak from the 1/f background.
Table\,\ref{tab:fooof} has the results.

I am still learning how to interpret FOOOF output, but here is what I
noticed so far.
Only 5 out of 11 channels had a detected alpha peak. I think the
clipping on the felt tEEG channels is probably hiding the alpha band
on those channels.
Ch9 (paste tEEG) and Ch10 (paste eEEG) both show alpha peaks around
12.7\,Hz and 10.8\,Hz, and those seem like the most reliable ones.
Ch10 and Ch11 had poor fits ($R^{2} = 0.80$ and $0.02$).
Ch11's fit is basically useless, probably because the clipping makes the
spectrum too noisy for FOOOF to fit properly.
Ch10 has a weird rising high-frequency tail that FOOOF does not seem to
handle well -- I am not sure what is causing that yet.

\section{Correlation with the Disc}

Fig.\,\ref{fig:corr} shows how well each channel's alpha envelope tracks
the disc electrode (Ch11).
The paste channels (Ch10, $r=0.968$ and Ch9, $r=0.936$) track it
very closely, which makes sense.
The felt tEEG channels have much lower correlation -- Ch1 is
basically zero ($r=0.006$).

From what I have read, I think the low correlation on the tEEG channels
is expected because the Laplacian is supposed to filter out the
widely spread alpha signal that the disc picks up.
So the tEEG channels should be showing more localized activity instead.
If that is the right way to think about it, then the low numbers
are actually a good sign -- but I wanted to check with you on that.

\section{Spectrogram SSIM: Image Comparison Between tEEG and eEEG}

This is the image processing comparison you suggested.
For each TCRE pair, I computed the SSIM~\cite{wang2004} between the tEEG and eEEG
spectrograms.
An SSIM of 1.0 means the two images are identical, and anything above
0.8 is considered high similarity.
All five pairs came out between 0.32 and 0.48, so there is a pretty
clear difference between the tEEG and eEEG spectrograms -- the
Laplacian seems to be changing the signal structure, not just scaling it.

Table\,\ref{tab:ssim} has the scores and
Figs.\,\ref{fig:ssim1}--\ref{fig:ssim5} show the full comparisons.
The difference maps all show tEEG having higher power than eEEG.
On the felt pairs I think this is mostly from the noise/impedance issue.
On the paste pair (Fig.\,\ref{fig:ssim5}), the difference is smoother
and more even, which I think is what you would expect from spatial
filtering rather than just noise.
I think this comparison will look better once we fix the impedance
on the felt electrodes.
