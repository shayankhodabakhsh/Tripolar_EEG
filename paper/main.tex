\abstract{The tripolar concentric ring electrode (TCRE) records two simultaneous derivations at one scalp site: a conventional outer-ring channel (eEEG) and an on-board surface-Laplacian estimate (tEEG). Existing TCRE studies use different scalp couplings---saline-soaked felt, conductive gel, paste---and different processing pipelines, which makes their results awkward to read against each other. This pilot compares the three couplings on the same hardware: a felt setup ($n=10$), a gel setup ($n=13$), and a paste TCRE present in both setups as a cross-setup bridge. Ring geometry and the on-board Laplacian are identical across both setups, so what differs across cohorts is electrolyte and scalp contact. Every recording is processed through one minimal pipeline (60~Hz notch, zero-phase FIR 0.05--55~Hz bandpass) and scored on three scale-invariant endpoints: alpha-band SNR, eyes-closed/eyes-open reactivity, and tEEG--eEEG spectrogram SSIM. The Berger effect is detectable on Paste tEEG in 21/23, Gel tEEG in 13/13, and Felt tEEG in 10/10. The construction effect shows up most cleanly in SSIM: per-class medians are Felt $0.31 <$ Gel $0.52 <$ Paste $0.71$, and the Felt-vs-Gel contrast is large ($U=0$, $p<0.001$, $d=-2.52$). The paste-TCRE bridge does not separate the two setups on any endpoint. We frame these findings as descriptive at the pilot scale.}

\keyword{tripolar concentric ring electrode; surface Laplacian; EEG; alpha rhythm; visual evoked potential; methods harmonization; electrolyte; signal quality}

\section{Introduction}

The tripolar concentric ring electrode (TCRE) places three concentric conductive rings at a single scalp site and produces two simultaneous derivations: an outer-ring conventional recording (eEEG) referenced to a remote site, and an on-board surface-Laplacian estimate computed from all three rings (tEEG)~\cite{besio2006}. The Laplacian acts as a spatial high-pass filter and has been shown to suppress volume conduction, sharpen focal source detection, and reduce reference-related artifact relative to disc EEG~\cite{besio2006,koka2007}.

A practical question for any TCRE study is how the device couples to the scalp. Three constructions are commonly used: saline-soaked felt pads~\cite{besio2006,toole2019hfo,liu2020spatial}, conductive gel~\cite{adeli2024,stuart2024sleep}, and conductive paste~\cite{koka2007,besio2008bci}. Each construction has trade-offs in preparation time, impedance stability, motion robustness, and patient tolerance~\cite{tallgren2005}. Published TCRE evaluations typically use a single construction and a study-specific processing pipeline~\cite{wise2025vep,stuart2024sleep,toole2019hfo}, which makes it difficult to read across reports and compare constructions on like-for-like endpoints.

The closest sibling work is Wise et al.~\cite{wise2025vep}, who recently compared TCRE and traditional EEG on the pattern-reversal visual evoked potential, recording both modalities simultaneously. Their question is whether the TCRE itself recovers a usable VEP relative to a conventional disc; ours is whether, given that TCREs work, the choice of electrolyte coupling changes the picture. The two questions are complementary, and our paste-tEEG VEP morphology in Section~\ref{sec:vep_scope} provides a small qualitative consistency check against their published recordings (LF02 paste-tEEG P100 at 109~ms is in the canonical adult range; full quantitative comparison against Wise et al.'s reported latencies and amplitudes is left to a follow-up).

This work is a methods-harmonized pilot comparison of three TCRE constructions---saline-soaked felt, conductive gel, and paste---processed through a single, deliberately minimal preprocessing pipeline and evaluated on scale-invariant endpoints. The paste TCRE is recorded in both acquisition setups and serves as a cross-setup bridge. The contribution is methodological: we document the harmonized pipeline, report cohort-level results for alpha-band detection, eyes-closed reactivity, spectrogram similarity, and (where the data support it) visual evoked potentials, and because both setups use the same hardware Laplacian on TCREs of identical ring geometry, the cross-construction contrast is on electrolyte and coupling rather than on the Laplacian estimator itself.

The framing throughout is descriptive and pilot-grade: cohort sizes are small, sample sizes per construction are imbalanced, and we make no causal claims. The aim is to give future TCRE comparison studies a shared analysis pipeline and a reusable cross-setup bridge design.

\subsection{Recording Setups}
The TCRE has three concentric conductive rings: an outer ring at radius $r_{o}$, a middle/inner ring at radius $r_{i}<r_{o}$, and a central disc. Let $V_{o}$, $V_{i}$, and $V_{d}$ be
   the potentials measured on each ring relative to a remote reference. The conventional (eEEG) channel is the outer-ring potential
  \begin{equation}
  V_{\mathrm{eEEG}} \;=\; V_{o}.
  \label{eq:eeeg}
  \end{equation}
  The on-board tEEG channel is the amplifier's hardware-implemented surface-Laplacian estimate~\cite{besio2006},
  \begin{equation}
  V_{\mathrm{tEEG}}
  \;=\;
  \alpha_{i}\,V_{i} \;-\; \alpha_{o}\,V_{o} \;-\; \alpha_{d}\,V_{d},
  \label{eq:teeg}
  \end{equation}
  where the weights $\alpha_{i},\alpha_{o},\alpha_{d}$ are set by the amplifier's analog front-end so that the linear combination approximates the discrete second spatial derivative on
   a flat scalp~\cite{besio2006,koka2007}. Both setups in this study record $V_{\mathrm{tEEG}}$ as a hardware output: no software Laplacian reconstruction from the three ring channels
  is performed.

Both setups record BrainVision INT16 binary data at 1000~Hz. Amplifier resolution is read directly from each subject's .vhdr at load time (0.1~$\upmu$V/bit on most sessions; 0.0488~$\upmu$V/bit on a subset of gel sessions recorded with a different amplifier model).

In both setups the tEEG channel is the amplifier's hardware Laplacian output, and the eEEG channel is the conventional outer-ring derivation referenced to a remote site. No software Laplacian reconstruction is performed.

No per-channel amplitude scaling is applied beyond the int16-to-microvolt conversion specified in each subject's .vhdr.\\
  Raw integer codes $K_{c}[n]\in\{-32{,}768,\ldots,32{,}767\}$ are converted to micro-volts at load time using the per-channel resolution $r_{c}$ ($\upmu$V/bit) read directly from the
  subject's \texttt{.vhdr}:
  \begin{equation}
  x_{c}[n] \;=\; K_{c}[n]\,\cdot\,r_{c}.
  \label{eq:adc}
  \end{equation}
  Most sessions use $r_{c}=0.1\ \upmu$V/bit (V-Amp); a subset of gel sessions recorded on a different amplifier (BrainAmp) use $r_{c}=0.0488\ \upmu$V/bit. No further per-channel
  amplitude scaling is applied.

\subsection{Preprocessing Pipeline}

The primary pipeline is intentionally minimal so that adaptive cleaners cannot behave differently across constructions and confound the comparison:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item 60~Hz IIR notch filter ($Q=30$). The analog prototype is
  \begin{equation}
  H_{\mathrm{notch}}(s) \;=\; \frac{s^{2} + \omega_{0}^{2}}{s^{2} + (\omega_{0}/Q)\,s + \omega_{0}^{2}},
  \qquad \omega_{0} = 2\pi f_{0},\; f_{0}=60~\mathrm{Hz},
  \label{eq:notch}
  \end{equation}
  which is mapped to a digital filter via the bilinear transform with sample rate $f_{s}=1000$~Hz. The $-3$~dB notch bandwidth is $\mathrm{BW} = f_{0}/Q = 2$~Hz.

 \item Zero-phase FIR bandpass 0.05--55~Hz, Hamming window. The bandpass impulse response is the Hamming-windowed difference of two ideal lowpass kernels:
  \begin{align}
  h[n] &= w[n]\bigl(2 f_{h}\,\mathrm{sinc}(2 f_{h} \tilde{n}) - 2 f_{l}\,\mathrm{sinc}(2 f_{l} \tilde{n})\bigr),
  \quad \tilde{n} = (n - (N-1)/2)/f_{s},\nonumber\\
  w[n] &= 0.54 - 0.46\cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{2\pi n}{N-1}\Bigr),\qquad n = 0,\ldots,N-1,
  \label{eq:fir}
  \end{align}
  with passband edges $f_{l}=0.05$~Hz, $f_{h}=55$~Hz and tap count $N$ chosen to span four cycles of $f_{l}$. Zero-phase response is achieved by forward--backward filtering:
  \begin{equation}
  y[n] \;=\; \bigl(h \ast \bigl(\overleftarrow{h \ast x}\bigr)\bigr)[n],
  \label{eq:filtfilt}
  \end{equation}
  which gives an effective magnitude response $|H_{\mathrm{eff}}(\omega)|^{2}$ and zero group delay.

\item No wavelet denoising. No ICA. No automated bad-channel interpolation. No re-referencing (the Laplacian is internal to each TCRE; the disc reference is the recorded mastoid).
\item Per-channel z-score normalization is applied only \emph{after} metric computation, never before SNR or PSD:
  \begin{equation}
  z_{c}[n] \;=\; \frac{x_{c}[n] - \mu_{c}}{\sigma_{c}},
  \qquad
  \mu_{c} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{n=0}^{T-1} x_{c}[n],\quad
  \sigma_{c}^{2} = \frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{n=0}^{T-1}\bigl(x_{c}[n]-\mu_{c}\bigr)^{2},
  \label{eq:zscore}
  \end{equation}
  where $c$ indexes channels and $T$ is the number of samples in the segment under test (per-epoch in variant~A, per-recording when reported as a normalised trace).
\end{enumerate}

Three sensitivity variants are reported in the supplement: (A)~notch + bandpass + Daubechies-8 level-3 wavelet + per-epoch z-score (the original gel-TCRE pipeline); (B)~notch + bandpass only (primary); (C)~notch only. Conclusions are required to hold across all three before any claim is retained in the main text.


\subsection{Quality Control and Exclusions}\label{sec:qc}
Each subject and each channel is screened on two computed quantities and three count-based integrity checks before entering the analysis cohort. The two computed quantities are the
ADC clipping fraction and the mean tEEG alpha SNR.

\paragraph{ADC clipping fraction.} For channel $c$ recorded with INT16 resolution $r_{c}$ ($\upmu$V/bit) and rail $K_{\max}=32{,}767$, the per-channel clipping fraction is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{clip}_{c}
\;=\;
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{n=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{1}\!\left\{\,\bigl|x_{c}[n]\bigr|\,/\,r_{c}\,\ge\,K_{\max}-1\,\right\},
\label{eq:clip}
\end{equation}
where $T$ is the recording length in samples and $\mathbb{1}\{\cdot\}$ is the indicator function. The per-subject clipping percentage reported in Table~\ref{tab:qc} is the mean
across all channels: $\overline{\mathrm{clip}} = (100/C)\sum_{c=1}^{C}\mathrm{clip}_{c}$.

\paragraph{Mean tEEG alpha SNR.} Let $\mathcal{T}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,C\}$ index the subject's tEEG channels (the four felt-tEEG plus the paste-tEEG channel for the felt setup; the
two gel-tEEG plus the paste-tEEG channel for the gel setup). The per-subject mean tEEG alpha SNR is
\begin{equation}
\overline{\mathrm{SNR}_{\alpha}^{\,t}}
\;=\;
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{T}}\mathrm{SNR}_{\alpha}^{(c)},
\label{eq:meansnrt}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{SNR}_{\alpha}^{(c)}$ defined as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:snr}. We use this aggregate, not any single channel, as the SNR-based QC gate so that one bad tEEG channel cannot fail
an otherwise good recording.
\paragraph{Decision rule.} Each subject is assigned a three-tier status using the gates in Table~\ref{tab:qc_rule}. The rule first checks four hard-fail conditions; a subject failing
 any one is excluded entirely. The rule then counts soft-flag conditions; a subject with exactly one soft flag is retained with a \textbf{WARN} status, and a subject with two or more
 soft flags is excluded.

\begin{table}[H]
\caption{QC decision rule. \texttt{n\_open}, \texttt{n\_close} are eyes-open and eyes-closed epoch counts; \texttt{dur} is the recording duration in seconds.}
\label{tab:qc_rule}
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{llc}
\toprule
\textbf{Type} & \textbf{Condition} & \textbf{Effect} \\
\midrule
Hard-fail  & $\overline{\mathrm{clip}} > 5\%$ & exclude subject \\
Hard-fail  & $\overline{\mathrm{SNR}_{\alpha}^{\,t}} < -1\ \mathrm{dB}$ & exclude subject \\
Hard-fail  & $\texttt{n\_open} < 2$ \;\textbf{or}\; $\texttt{n\_close} < 2$ & exclude subject \\
Hard-fail  & $\texttt{dur} < 60\ \mathrm{s}$ & exclude subject \\
Hard-fail  & unknown channel-layout (e.g.\ 9-channel prototype) & exclude subject \\
\midrule
Soft-flag  & $2\% < \overline{\mathrm{clip}} \le 5\%$ & WARN \\
Soft-flag  & $-1\ \mathrm{dB} \le \overline{\mathrm{SNR}_{\alpha}^{\,t}} < 0\ \mathrm{dB}$ & WARN \\
\midrule
Aggregator & $\ge 2$ soft flags simultaneously & exclude subject \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The status field in Table~\ref{tab:qc} reports \textbf{PASS} for subjects with no flags, \textbf{WARN} for subjects with exactly one soft flag (retained in primary analyses;
supplement Section~S2 reports a PASS-only sensitivity check), and the row is omitted for excluded subjects. The exclusion list (subjects rejected by the hard-fail gates) is reported
in Supplement Section~S2.

A separate per-channel rule applies inside otherwise-PASS subjects: any individual channel with $\mathrm{clip}_{c}>5\%$ is dropped from group statistics for that subject only, while
the rest of the recording is retained.

\subsection{Endpoints}

We report only \emph{scale-invariant} endpoints in the main cross-construction comparison. Raw amplitudes are not directly comparable across the two acquisition setups because the felt and gel cohorts were recorded on different amplifier models with different INT16 resolutions (V-Amp at $0.1\ \upmu$V/bit on most sessions, BrainAmp at $0.0488\ \upmu$V/bit on a subset of gel sessions; see Eq.~\eqref{eq:adc}) and on different days with potentially different operators. No per-channel amplitude scaling is applied beyond the per-recording resolution conversion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:adc}; the alpha-band SNR, eyes-closed/eyes-open reactivity, and tEEG--eEEG SSIM endpoints are all dimensionless ratios and therefore unaffected by between-setup amplitude differences.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Alpha-band SNR (dB)} computed from the Welch PSD $\hat{S}(f)$~\cite{welch1967}:
  \begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SNR}_{\alpha}\,\bigl[\mathrm{dB}\bigr]
  \;=\;
  10\log_{10}\!\frac{P_{\alpha}}{P_{\mathrm{nbr}}},
  \quad
  P_{\alpha}=\!\int_{8}^{13}\!\hat{S}(f)\,df,
  \quad
  P_{\mathrm{nbr}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\Bigl[\!\int_{3}^{8}\hat{S}(f)\,df + \!\int_{13}^{18}\hat{S}(f)\,df\Bigr].
  \label{eq:snr}
  \end{equation}

  The Welch PSD~\cite{welch1967} averages periodograms of $K$ overlapping Hamming-windowed segments of length $L=n_{\mathrm{per\,seg}}$ with $50\%$ overlap:
  \begin{equation}
  \hat{S}(f)
  \;=\;
  \frac{1}{K \cdot L \cdot U}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Bigl|\sum_{n=0}^{L-1}w[n]\,x_{k}[n]\,e^{-j2\pi f n/f_{s}}\Bigr|^{2},
  \label{eq:welch}
  \end{equation}
  where $w[n]$ is the Hamming window, $U=(1/L)\sum_{n}w[n]^{2}$ is the window normalisation, $x_{k}$ is the $k$-th windowed segment, and $L=4096$ for the SNR endpoints and $L=2048$ for
   the spectrograms used as SSIM inputs.
\item \textbf{Alpha reactivity} is the closed/open ratio
  \begin{equation}
  R_{\alpha} \;=\; \frac{P_{\alpha,\mathrm{closed}}}{P_{\alpha,\mathrm{open}}},
  \label{eq:reactivity}
  \end{equation}
  with $P_{\alpha,\cdot}$ defined as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:snr} and computed separately on the eyes-closed and eyes-open epochs. Values $R_{\alpha}>1$ indicate the Berger
  effect~\cite{kirschfeld2005}.
\item \textbf{Spectrogram SSIM}~\cite{wang2004} between the per-pair tEEG spectrogram $X$ and eEEG spectrogram $Y$ (each computed with $n_{\mathrm{per\,seg}}=2048$,
  $f_{\max}=45$~Hz):
  \begin{equation}
  \mathrm{SSIM}(X,Y)
  \;=\;
  \frac{(2\mu_{X}\mu_{Y}+c_{1})(2\sigma_{XY}+c_{2})}
       {(\mu_{X}^{2}+\mu_{Y}^{2}+c_{1})(\sigma_{X}^{2}+\sigma_{Y}^{2}+c_{2})},
  \label{eq:ssim}
  \end{equation}
  where $\mu$, $\sigma^{2}$ and $\sigma_{XY}$ are the local mean, variance and covariance over an $11\times11$ Gaussian window, and $c_{1}=(0.01\,L)^{2}$, $c_{2}=(0.03\,L)^{2}$ are
  stabilising constants with $L$ the dynamic range. SSIM is dimensionless and bounded on $[0,1]$, hence directly cross-setup comparable.


\item \textbf{Visual evoked potential (Felt setup only)}: peak latencies and amplitudes of the pre-averaged checkerboard response, reported for the two long-felt subjects whose .avg files are available in this study (LF02, LF03). For each canonical component $C\in\{\mathrm{N75},\mathrm{P100},\mathrm{N135}\}$ with search window $W_{C}=[t_{C,\mathrm{lo}},\,t_{C,\mathrm{hi}}]$, the reported latency $\hat{t}_{C}$ is
  \begin{equation}
  \hat{t}_{C}
  \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
  \arg\min_{t\in W_{C}}\,\bar{V}(t) & C\in\{\mathrm{N75},\mathrm{N135}\},\\[2pt]
  \arg\max_{t\in W_{C}}\,\bar{V}(t) & C=\mathrm{P100},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:peak}
  \end{equation}
  with $\bar{V}(t)$ the channel-mean grand-average evoked response. Search windows: $W_{\mathrm{N75}}=[50,95]$, $W_{\mathrm{P100}}=[90,140]$, $W_{\mathrm{N135}}=[125,180]$~(ms; values set in the accompanying build script). A latency that pins at the lower or upper boundary of $W_{C}$ is reported as ``n.r.'' (not resolved) rather than as a numerical peak.\\
  VEPs from the gel cohort are excluded from cross-construction VEP statistics because the gel-era acquisition produced unreliable evoked responses under the harmonized pipeline; see Section~\ref{sec:vep_scope} for scope of this exclusion and Section~\ref{sec:gel_vep_sanity} for the qualitative documentation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}

The subject is the unit of analysis throughout. For each metric we aggregate per subject and per channel class (FELT\_TEEG, GEL\_TEEG, PASTE\_TEEG, FELT\_EEEG, GEL\_EEEG, PASTE\_EEEG, DISC). The choice of test depends on whether a contrast is paired or independent. Within a setup, cross-construction contrasts---Felt-tEEG vs.\ Paste-tEEG inside the felt cohort, Gel-tEEG vs.\ Paste-tEEG inside the gel cohort, and the eEEG counterparts---are paired by subject, because both constructions sit on the same head-cap during the same recording; we test these with the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test and report Cohen's $d_z$ together with the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation $r$. Between-setup contrasts---Felt-tEEG vs.\ Gel-tEEG, and the paste-TCRE bridge between the felt and gel setups---are independent samples and use the two-sided Mann--Whitney U test with Cohen's $d$.


\begin{equation}
  W = \min\!\bigl(R_{+},R_{-}\bigr),
  \label{eq:wilcoxon}
  \end{equation}
  with $R_{\pm}$ as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rrb}. The Mann--Whitney $U$ for independent samples $A$, $B$ is
  \begin{equation}
  U
  \;=\;
  \sum_{i \in A}\sum_{j \in B}
  \bigl[\,\mathbb{1}\{x_{i}>y_{j}\} + \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{1}\{x_{i}=y_{j}\}\bigr].
  \label{eq:mwu}
  \end{equation}
  Two-sided $p$-values are computed from the exact distribution when $n\le 25$ and from the normal approximation otherwise; both are produced by \texttt{scipy.stats.wilcoxon} and
  \texttt{scipy.stats.mannwhitneyu} (SciPy 1.x~\cite{virtanen2020}).


For a paired contrast with by-subject differences $d_{i}=a_{i}-b_{i}$, $i=1,\ldots,n$, we report Cohen's $d_{z}$ together with the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation:
  \begin{equation}
  d_{z}
  \;=\;
  \frac{\bar{d}}{s_{d}},
  \qquad
  \bar{d}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} d_{i},
  \quad
  s_{d}^{2} = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(d_{i}-\bar{d}\bigr)^{2},
  \label{eq:dz}
  \end{equation}
  \begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{rb}}
  \;=\;
  \frac{R_{+} - R_{-}}{R_{+} + R_{-}},
  \qquad
  R_{\pm} = \!\!\!\sum_{i:\,\mathrm{sign}(d_{i})=\pm}\!\!\!\mathrm{rank}\bigl(|d_{i}|\bigr),
  \label{eq:rrb}
  \end{equation}
  where ranks are over the nonzero $|d_{i}|$. For an independent contrast between groups $A$ ($n_{A}$ subjects) and $B$ ($n_{B}$), we report Cohen's $d$:
  \begin{equation}
  d
  \;=\;
  \frac{\bar{x}_{A} - \bar{x}_{B}}{s_{p}},
  \qquad
  s_{p}^{2}
  =
  \frac{(n_{A}-1)s_{A}^{2} + (n_{B}-1)s_{B}^{2}}{n_{A}+n_{B}-2}.
  \label{eq:cohend}
  \end{equation}
For each per-class metric we report the median together with a bootstrap bias-corrected and accelerated (BCa) 95\% confidence interval~\cite{efron1987} computed from $B=10{,}000$
  resamples drawn with replacement at the subject level. Writing $\hat{\theta}=\mathrm{median}(x)$ and $\{\hat{\theta}^{*}_{b}\}_{b=1}^{B}$ for the bootstrap distribution, the BCa
  endpoints are the $\alpha_{1}$ and $\alpha_{2}$ quantiles of $\{\hat{\theta}^{*}_{b}\}$ with
  \begin{equation}
  \alpha_{j}
  \;=\;
  \Phi\!\left(\hat{z}_{0} + \frac{\hat{z}_{0}+z_{(j)}}{1 - \hat{a}\bigl(\hat{z}_{0}+z_{(j)}\bigr)}\right),
  \quad j\in\{1,2\},
  \label{eq:bca}
  \end{equation}
  where $\Phi$ is the standard-normal CDF, $z_{(1)}=z_{0.025}$ and $z_{(2)}=z_{0.975}$ are the 95\% quantiles,
  $\hat{z}_{0}=\Phi^{-1}\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{B}\#\{\hat{\theta}^{*}_{b}<\hat{\theta}\}\bigr)$ is the bias-correction term, and $\hat{a}$ is the acceleration estimated by jackknife as
  in~\cite{efron1987}. Random seed is fixed at $0$ in \texttt{scripts/build\_paper\_stats.py} so that re-running the script reproduces the reported intervals exactly.

\subsection{Eyes-Closed/Eyes-Open Reactivity}
Figure~\ref{fig:reactivity} shows alpha reactivity (closed/open ratio); values above 1 are read as a Berger effect~\cite{kirschfeld2005}. The per-class medians (BCa 95\% CI) are Felt tEEG $1.49$ $[1.10,\ 1.87]$ ($n=10$); Gel tEEG $3.63$ $[1.64,\ 5.32]$ ($n=13$); Paste tEEG $3.43$ $[2.30,\ 6.88]$ ($n=23$); and disc $4.72$ $[2.41,\ 8.62]$ ($n=23$). The Berger effect itself is detected on Felt tEEG in 10/10 subjects, Gel tEEG in 13/13, and Paste tEEG in 21/23. The between-setup contrast Felt-tEEG vs.\ Gel-tEEG separates the two cohorts ($U=26$, $p=0.017$, $d=-1.11$, Mann--Whitney): gel reactivity is meaningfully higher than felt reactivity. The within-setup paired contrasts are smaller---Felt-tEEG vs.\ Paste-tEEG, $W=5$, $p=0.020$, $d_z=-0.58$ ($n_{\mathrm{pairs}}=10$); Gel-tEEG vs.\ Paste-tEEG, $W=23$, $p=0.13$, $d_z=-0.43$ ($n_{\mathrm{pairs}}=13$).

The two paste-tEEG subjects below the Berger threshold are SF02 (paste-tEEG reactivity 0.96; disc 5.00; felt-tEEG 1.67) and SF03 (paste-tEEG 0.88; disc 0.96; felt-tEEG 1.10). On SF03 the attenuation is visible across all three derivations, which points to a subject-level rather than a construction-level effect. On SF02 the non-response is local to the paste-tEEG channel while the disc and felt-tEEG show the expected modulation, suggesting a placement-level issue on the paste TCRE during that session; SF02 also carries a $3.3\%$ ADC-clip WARN flag (Table~\ref{tab:qc}). Both subjects remain in the cohort with the non-response flagged here rather than silently dropped.

The Felt-tEEG reactivity median (1.49) sits well below the Gel-tEEG median (3.63), which warrants comment. Most directly, ADC clipping is concentrated in the felt cohort---four of ten felt sessions (LF02, SF01, SF02, SF07; Table~\ref{tab:qc}) carry a clip-percentage flag, and the rail-clipping events tend to land on high-amplitude eyes-closed segments where they depress $P_{\alpha,\mathrm{closed}}$ and therefore the closed/open ratio. The same clipping mechanism does not symmetrically depress the alpha-band SNR (Felt-tEEG $1.72$~dB vs.\ Gel-tEEG $1.43$~dB; broadly comparable), because the SNR endpoint compares alpha-band power to flanking-band power within the \emph{same} epoch, so a rail-induced cap on the eyes-closed alpha peak attenuates numerator and denominator of $P_{\alpha}/P_{\mathrm{nbr}}$ together; reactivity, by contrast, divides closed-condition alpha power by open-condition alpha power, and rail clipping during high-amplitude closed segments bites the numerator far harder than the denominator. The reactivity-vs-SNR asymmetry is therefore expected from the clipping pattern alone. Closed-condition power is also estimated from fewer epochs in the felt cohort, so the per-subject estimate is noisier. A residual construction-level effect remains possible: saline can spread beyond the nominal ring footprint and lower the effective interface impedance, both of which would attenuate posterior alpha at the scalp. The take-away is that the Berger effect itself survives in 10/10 felt subjects, so we keep the felt cohort in the main analysis, but the absolute reactivity magnitude on Felt-tEEG should be read as confounded with acquisition quality.

\subsection{Paste-TCRE Cross-Setup Bridge}
The paste TCRE is recorded in both setups, so its between-setup behaviour acts as a sanity check on residual cross-setup variance that is not attributable to construction. Figure~\ref{fig:paste_bridge} shows the three endpoints on paste TCRE in the felt setup vs.\ the gel setup. None of the three Mann--Whitney contrasts separate the two cohorts at conventional thresholds (alpha SNR $U=89$, $p=0.15$, $d=+0.51$; reactivity $U=46$, $p=0.25$, $d=-0.39$; SSIM $U=69$, $p=0.83$, $d=+0.01$; $n_{\mathrm{felt}}=10$, $n_{\mathrm{gel}}=13$). At these sample sizes the bridge has roughly 60\% power against a Cohen's $d$ of $0.8$, so we cannot rule out smaller between-setup effects.   For the paste-bridge contrast (Mann--Whitney U with $n_{\mathrm{felt}}=10$, $n_{\mathrm{gel}}=13$), the asymptotic power against an alternative with standardised effect size $d$ is
  \begin{equation}
  1-\beta
  \;=\;
  \Phi\!\left(
  -z_{1-\alpha/2} +
  d\sqrt{\frac{n_{\mathrm{felt}}\,n_{\mathrm{gel}}}{n_{\mathrm{felt}}+n_{\mathrm{gel}}}}
  \right),
  \label{eq:power}
  \end{equation}
  which gives $1-\beta\approx 0.60$ at $d=0.8$ and $\alpha=0.05$. Smaller between-setup effects cannot be ruled out at the present sample sizes. What the bridge does rule out within a given metric is a between-setup effect of the magnitude we see across constructions: paste-bridge SSIM is essentially null ($d=+0.01$, $p=0.83$), whereas the Felt-vs-Gel SSIM construction contrast is $d=-2.52$ on the same metric. The construction-level SSIM separation is more than two orders of magnitude larger than the corresponding setup-level effect, so the cross-construction SSIM finding is unlikely to be driven by acquisition-side differences between the two setups.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Methods Harmonization}
The strongest contribution of this pilot is the harmonized pipeline itself. By excluding adaptive cleaners (ICA, ASR, automated bad-channel interpolation) and keeping the only mandatory steps a notch and a linear-phase bandpass, we ensure that any between-construction differences are not artifacts of a cleaner behaving differently on clean paste vs.\ noisier felt. The cost of this choice is that subjects with bad channels stay flagged rather than rescued, which we report transparently in QC.

\subsection{Felt-tEEG vs.\ Gel-tEEG: Same Estimator, Same Geometry}
Both setups use the same hardware Laplacian on TCREs of identical ring geometry, so the cross-construction contrast in §3 is on construction (electrolyte and coupling: saline-soaked felt vs.\ conductive gel vs.\ paste) rather than on estimator.

The most striking single number in §3 is the SSIM ordering Felt $0.31 <$ Gel $0.52 <$ Paste $0.71$, with very large effect sizes on both Felt-vs-Gel and Felt-vs-Paste. The temptation is to read this as ``felt is the most aggressive spatial filter,'' but a few caveats keep us from that reading. SSIM does not separate two channels that carry genuinely independent neural information from two channels that share a common neural signal corrupted by independent noise; both produce low SSIM. The alpha-SNR ordering does not mirror the SSIM ordering either---paste is the cleanest channel by SNR, while felt and gel sit close together---which makes the ``felt-is-cleanest-Laplacian'' reading awkward at best. Several electrolyte-level effects could plausibly drive the contrast we see. Saline can spread beyond the nominal ring footprint, effectively enlarging the outer-ring contact area; the three rings can carry asymmetric impedances, which would push construction-dependent imbalances into the on-board Laplacian; and each ring contributes its own contact noise. Per-ring impedance logging in a future cohort is what would actually discriminate between these. For now we read the SSIM ordering as a hypothesis-generating observation about electrolyte coupling, not as a verdict on which construction is the better spatial filter.

\subsection{Disc-EEG Alpha SNR Exceeds tEEG Alpha SNR}
Disc EEG has the highest alpha SNR of any class in our cohort (median 4.33~dB). On its face this looks unflattering for the Laplacian, which is supposed to improve focal-source SNR by rejecting volume-conducted broadband activity~\cite{besio2006,koka2007}. The standard surface-Laplacian explanation applies here: posterior alpha during the eyes-closed condition is broadly distributed across occipital cortex rather than focal, so the same spatial high-pass that helps a focal source rejects much of a diffuse one. We report the disc--tEEG SNR contrast for context, not as evidence against the TCRE.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item Pilot-scale cohort. Felt is $n=10$ (3 long-felt, 7 short-felt) and gel is $n=13$ after QC; any single-subject outlier visibly moves the medians and inferential claims should be read as preliminary.
\item ADC clipping was prevalent on early felt sessions (impedance issues). Per-channel exclusions retain the rest of each affected subject but reduce per-class effective $n$ for some channels.
\item Paste-TCRE recordings in the two setups were collected on different days and likely by different operators. The cross-setup bridge therefore estimates an upper bound on construction effects, not a strict null.
\item VEPs are reported only for the felt setup. The gel-era VEP anomaly is not yet root-caused and is documented qualitatively in the supplement.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What This Enables}
For practitioners, this paper offers a single open pipeline and a cross-setup bridge design that other TCRE labs can re-use to make their results readable against ours. We deliberately do not recommend one construction over another on the basis of this pilot; the contribution is the comparison framework, not a winner.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusions}

We presented a pilot, methods-harmonized comparison of three TCRE constructions (saline-soaked felt, conductive gel, paste) using a single minimal preprocessing pipeline and scale-invariant endpoints. Across constructions, alpha-band detection and reactivity were physiologically plausible, the paste-TCRE cross-setup bridge produced no significant between-setup differences ($n_{\mathrm{felt}}=10$, $n_{\mathrm{gel}}=13$), and tEEG vs.\ eEEG spectrograms differed structurally and in a construction-dependent way (median SSIM Felt~$0.31 < $ Gel~$0.52 < $ Paste~$0.71$, with Felt vs Gel and Felt vs Paste both reaching $p\leq 0.002$). Conclusions are descriptive given the small and imbalanced cohort. Future work will (i)~grow both cohorts beyond pilot scale, especially balancing the long-felt arm, and (ii)~re-collect a gel-setup VEP dataset on a single amplifier under the harmonized pipeline.
