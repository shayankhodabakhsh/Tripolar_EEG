\abstract{The tripolar concentric ring electrode (TCRE) records two simultaneous derivations at one scalp site: a conventional outer-ring channel (eEEG) and an on-board surface-Laplacian estimate (tEEG). Existing TCRE studies use different scalp couplings---saline-soaked felt, conductive gel, paste---and different processing pipelines, which makes their results awkward to read against each other. This pilot compares the three couplings on the same hardware: a felt setup ($n=10$), a gel setup ($n=13$), and a paste TCRE present in both setups as a cross-setup bridge. Ring geometry and the on-board Laplacian are identical across both setups, so what differs across cohorts is electrolyte and scalp contact. Every recording is processed through one minimal pipeline (60~Hz notch, zero-phase FIR 0.05--55~Hz bandpass) and scored on three scale-invariant endpoints: alpha-band SNR, eyes-closed/eyes-open reactivity, and tEEG--eEEG spectrogram SSIM. The Berger effect is detectable on Paste tEEG in 21/23, Gel tEEG in 13/13, and Felt tEEG in 10/10. The construction effect shows up most cleanly in SSIM: per-class medians are Felt $0.31 <$ Gel $0.52 <$ Paste $0.71$, and the Felt-vs-Gel contrast is large ($U=0$, $p<0.001$, $d=-2.52$). The paste-TCRE bridge does not separate the two setups on any endpoint. We frame these findings as descriptive at the pilot scale.}

\keyword{tripolar concentric ring electrode; surface Laplacian; EEG; alpha rhythm; visual evoked potential; methods harmonization; electrolyte; signal quality}

\section{Introduction}

The tripolar concentric ring electrode (TCRE) places three concentric conductive rings at a single scalp site and produces two simultaneous derivations: an outer-ring conventional recording (eEEG) referenced to a remote site, and an on-board surface-Laplacian estimate computed from all three rings (tEEG)~\cite{besio2006}. The Laplacian acts as a spatial high-pass filter and has been shown to suppress volume conduction, sharpen focal source detection, and reduce reference-related artifact relative to disc EEG~\cite{besio2006,koka2007}.

A practical question for any TCRE study is how the device couples to the scalp. Three constructions are commonly used: saline-soaked felt pads~\cite{besio2006}\,\TBD{add 1--2 additional felt-TCRE refs (Besio-lab papers using felt; Makeyev/Boudet if relevant)}, conductive gel~\cite{adeli2024}\,\TBD{add 1--2 additional gel-TCRE refs (any other lab paper using gel coupling)}, and conductive paste~\cite{koka2007}\,\TBD{add 1--2 additional paste-TCRE refs (Besio-lab or clinical TCRE work using paste)}. Each construction has trade-offs in preparation time, impedance stability, motion robustness, and patient tolerance \TBD{cite an electrolyte / skin--electrode-coupling review (e.g.\ Tallgren et al.\ 2005, or a more recent review of EEG electrode coupling)}. Published TCRE evaluations typically use a single construction and a study-specific processing pipeline \TBD{cite 2--3 representative single-construction TCRE evaluations to illustrate this point}, which makes it difficult to read across reports and compare constructions on like-for-like endpoints.

This work is a methods-harmonized pilot comparison of three TCRE constructions---saline-soaked felt, conductive gel, and paste---processed through a single, deliberately minimal preprocessing pipeline and evaluated on scale-invariant endpoints. The paste TCRE is recorded in both acquisition setups and serves as a cross-setup bridge. The contribution is methodological: we document the harmonized pipeline, report cohort-level results for alpha-band detection, eyes-closed reactivity, spectrogram similarity, and (where the data support it) visual evoked potentials, and because both setups use the same hardware Laplacian on TCREs of identical ring geometry, the cross-construction contrast is on electrolyte and coupling rather than on the Laplacian estimator itself.

The framing throughout is descriptive and pilot-grade: cohort sizes are small, sample sizes per construction are imbalanced, and we make no causal claims. The aim is to give future TCRE comparison studies a shared analysis pipeline and a reusable cross-setup bridge design.

\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Subjects and Cohort}

The cohort comprises three TCRE construction classes recorded across two acquisition setups (Table~\ref{tab:cohort}).

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Felt setup} (URI lab, 2026). Eleven-channel BrainVision recording at 1000~Hz: four felt TCREs (channels 1--8, alternating tEEG/eEEG), one paste TCRE (channels 9--10), and a paste conventional disc (channel 11). The cohort comprises all felt sessions (long and short) that pass per-subject signal-quality QC as specified in Section~\ref{sec:qc}. Felt sessions divide into two physical sub-cohorts by the screw used to seat the TCRE in its 3D-printed housing. Long-felt (LF) sessions use the longer-screw housing, which seats a thicker saline-soaked felt pad and supports a longer recording window; short-felt (SF) sessions use the shorter-screw housing. The two sub-cohorts are pooled for the primary analyses in Section~\ref{sec:cohort_qc}--§3.6, and supplement Section~S6 verifies that the construction-level conclusions hold in each sub-cohort separately.
\item \textbf{Gel setup} (URI lab, October--November 2024). Seven-channel BrainVision recording at 1000~Hz: two gel TCREs at O1 and O2 (channels 1--4), one paste TCRE at Pz (channels 5--6), and a conventional disc at Pz (channel 7). Cohort: all gel subjects discoverable under the project gel directory that pass per-subject QC (Section~\ref{sec:qc}).
\item \textbf{Paste TCRE bridge.} Paste TCRE is present in both setups and serves as the cross-setup comparability check.
\end{itemize}

All sessions were recorded under a checkerboard-VEP plus eyes-open/eyes-closed paradigm. The eyes-open/closed protocol follows~\cite{kirschfeld2005}.

\subsection{Recording Setups}

Both setups record BrainVision INT16 binary data at 1000~Hz. Amplifier resolution is read directly from each subject's .vhdr at load time (0.1~$\upmu$V/bit on most sessions; 0.0488~$\upmu$V/bit on a subset of gel sessions recorded with a different amplifier model).

In both setups the tEEG channel is the amplifier's hardware Laplacian output, and the eEEG channel is the conventional outer-ring derivation referenced to a remote site. No software Laplacian reconstruction is performed.

No per-channel amplitude scaling is applied beyond the int16-to-microvolt conversion specified in each subject's .vhdr.

\subsection{Preprocessing Pipeline}

The primary pipeline is intentionally minimal so that adaptive cleaners cannot behave differently across constructions and confound the comparison:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item 60~Hz IIR notch filter ($Q=30$).
\item Zero-phase FIR bandpass 0.05--55~Hz, Hamming window.
\item No wavelet denoising. No ICA. No automated bad-channel interpolation. No re-referencing (the Laplacian is internal to each TCRE; the disc reference is the recorded mastoid).
\item Per-channel z-score normalization is applied only \emph{after} metric computation, never before SNR or PSD.
\end{enumerate}

Three sensitivity variants are reported in the supplement: (A)~notch + bandpass + Daubechies-8 level-3 wavelet + per-epoch z-score (the original gel-TCRE pipeline); (B)~notch + bandpass only (primary); (C)~notch only. Conclusions are required to hold across all three before any claim is retained in the main text.

\subsection{Quality Control and Exclusions}\label{sec:qc}

QC gates are applied per subject and per channel:

\begin{itemize}
\item Channels with $>5\%$ ADC clipping are excluded from group statistics for that subject; the subject is retained for unaffected channels.
\item Subjects with fewer than two eyes-open or two eyes-closed epochs are excluded entirely.
\item Subjects with unknown channel layouts (e.g.\ early nine-channel felt prototypes without a registered config) are excluded.
\end{itemize}

Exclusion counts, reasons, and per-subject QC tables are reported in Section~\ref{sec:cohort_qc} and the supplement.

\subsection{Endpoints}

We report only \emph{scale-invariant} endpoints in the main cross-construction comparison, because the gel setup applies a hardware-level $1/187$ scaling on tEEG channels that makes raw amplitudes incomparable to the felt setup.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Alpha-band SNR (dB)}: $10\log_{10}(P_{\alpha}/P_{\mathrm{neighbor}})$, with $P_\alpha$ over 8--13~Hz and $P_{\mathrm{neighbor}}$ from flanking sub-bands of equal width, computed from the Welch PSD~\cite{welch1967} ($n_{\mathrm{per\,seg}}=4096$).
\item \textbf{Alpha reactivity}: $P_{\alpha,\mathrm{closed}}/P_{\alpha,\mathrm{open}}$; values $>1$ indicate the Berger effect~\cite{kirschfeld2005}.
\item \textbf{Spectrogram SSIM}: structural similarity~\cite{wang2004} between the per-pair tEEG and eEEG spectrograms ($n_{\mathrm{per\,seg}}=2048$, $f_{\max}=45$~Hz). SSIM is dimensionless and directly cross-setup comparable.
\item \textbf{Visual evoked potential (Felt setup only)}: peak-to-peak amplitude of the pre-averaged checkerboard response, reported for the two long-felt subjects whose .avg files are available in this study (LF02, LF03). VEPs from the gel cohort are excluded from the present analysis because the gel-era acquisition produced unreliable evoked responses under the harmonized pipeline; see Section~\ref{sec:vep_scope} for scope of this exclusion.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}

The subject is the unit of analysis throughout. For each metric we aggregate per subject and per channel class (FELT\_TEEG, GEL\_TEEG, PASTE\_TEEG, FELT\_EEEG, GEL\_EEEG, PASTE\_EEEG, DISC). The choice of test depends on whether a contrast is paired or independent. Within a setup, cross-construction contrasts---Felt-tEEG vs.\ Paste-tEEG inside the felt cohort, Gel-tEEG vs.\ Paste-tEEG inside the gel cohort, and the eEEG counterparts---are paired by subject, because both constructions sit on the same head-cap during the same recording; we test these with the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test and report Cohen's $d_z$ together with the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation $r$. Between-setup contrasts---Felt-tEEG vs.\ Gel-tEEG, and the paste-TCRE bridge between the felt and gel setups---are independent samples and use the two-sided Mann--Whitney U test with Cohen's $d$. Central tendency is reported as the median with bootstrap BCa 95\% confidence intervals at 10{,}000 resamples. We report effect sizes and confidence intervals alongside $p$-values, and we do not adjust for multiple comparisons because the analysis is descriptive and the three endpoints are pre-specified.

\subsection{Alpha-Band Detection (SNR)}
Figure~\ref{fig:alpha_snr} shows alpha-band SNR by electrode class. The per-class medians (BCa 95\% CI) are Felt tEEG $1.72$~dB $[0.93,\ 2.31]$ ($n=10$); Gel tEEG $1.43$~dB $[0.19,\ 3.42]$ ($n=13$); Paste tEEG $2.97$~dB $[2.09,\ 5.65]$ ($n=23$); and disc $4.33$~dB $[2.91,\ 6.99]$ ($n=23$). The within-setup paired contrasts are Felt-tEEG vs.\ Paste-tEEG, $W=1$, $p=0.004$, $d_z=-1.34$ ($n_{\mathrm{pairs}}=10$), and Gel-tEEG vs.\ Paste-tEEG, $W=25$, $p=0.17$, $d_z=-0.41$ ($n_{\mathrm{pairs}}=13$). The between-setup contrast Felt-tEEG vs.\ Gel-tEEG is essentially flat ($U=68$, $p=0.88$, $d=-0.00$; $n_a=10$, $n_b=13$). Alpha sits above the flanking neighbour bands on every class, which is what we expect from posterior alpha in the eyes-closed condition. Paste-TCRE has the highest alpha SNR of the three constructions; Felt and Gel are broadly comparable.

\subsection{Eyes-Closed/Eyes-Open Reactivity}
Figure~\ref{fig:reactivity} shows alpha reactivity (closed/open ratio); values above 1 are read as a Berger effect~\cite{kirschfeld2005}. The per-class medians (BCa 95\% CI) are Felt tEEG $1.49$ $[1.10,\ 1.87]$ ($n=10$); Gel tEEG $3.63$ $[1.64,\ 5.32]$ ($n=13$); Paste tEEG $3.43$ $[2.30,\ 6.88]$ ($n=23$); and disc $4.72$ $[2.41,\ 8.62]$ ($n=23$). The Berger effect itself is detected on Felt tEEG in 10/10 subjects, Gel tEEG in 13/13, and Paste tEEG in 21/23. The between-setup contrast Felt-tEEG vs.\ Gel-tEEG separates the two cohorts ($U=26$, $p=0.017$, $d=-1.11$, Mann--Whitney): gel reactivity is meaningfully higher than felt reactivity. The within-setup paired contrasts are smaller---Felt-tEEG vs.\ Paste-tEEG, $W=5$, $p=0.020$, $d_z=-0.58$ ($n_{\mathrm{pairs}}=10$); Gel-tEEG vs.\ Paste-tEEG, $W=23$, $p=0.13$, $d_z=-0.43$ ($n_{\mathrm{pairs}}=13$).

The two paste-tEEG subjects below the Berger threshold are SF02 (paste-tEEG reactivity 0.96; disc 5.00; felt-tEEG 1.67) and SF03 (paste-tEEG 0.88; disc 0.96; felt-tEEG 1.10). On SF03 the attenuation is visible across all three derivations, which points to a subject-level rather than a construction-level effect. On SF02 the non-response is local to the paste-tEEG channel while the disc and felt-tEEG show the expected modulation, suggesting a placement-level issue on the paste TCRE during that session; SF02 also carries a $3.3\%$ ADC-clip WARN flag (Table~\ref{tab:qc}). Both subjects remain in the cohort with the non-response flagged here rather than silently dropped.

The Felt-tEEG reactivity median (1.49) sits well below the Gel-tEEG median (3.63), which warrants comment. Most directly, ADC clipping is concentrated in the felt cohort---four of ten felt sessions (LF02, SF01, SF02, SF07; Table~\ref{tab:qc}) carry a clip-percentage flag, and the rail-clipping events tend to land on high-amplitude eyes-closed segments where they depress $P_{\alpha,\mathrm{closed}}$ and therefore the closed/open ratio. Closed-condition power is also estimated from fewer epochs in the felt cohort, so the per-subject estimate is noisier. A residual construction-level effect remains possible: saline can spread beyond the nominal ring footprint and lower the effective interface impedance, both of which would attenuate posterior alpha at the scalp. The take-away is that the Berger effect itself survives in 10/10 felt subjects, so we keep the felt cohort in the main analysis, but the absolute reactivity magnitude on Felt-tEEG should be read as confounded with acquisition quality.

\subsection{Spectrogram SSIM Between Paired tEEG and eEEG}
Figure~\ref{fig:ssim} shows the per-pair SSIM between tEEG and eEEG spectrograms. SSIM is bounded on $[0,1]$ and dimensionless, so values are directly comparable across the two acquisition setups. The per-class medians (BCa 95\% CI) are Felt $0.307$ $[0.266,\ 0.338]$ ($n=10$); Gel $0.519$ $[0.470,\ 0.694]$ ($n=13$); Paste $0.710$ $[0.619,\ 0.758]$ ($n=23$). All three constructions sit well below the 0.8 conventional high-similarity threshold, so tEEG and eEEG cannot be treated as scaled copies of each other on any of the three couplings. The construction effect itself is large. Between setups, Felt-tEEG vs.\ Gel-tEEG differs at $U=0$, $p<0.001$, $d=-2.52$ (Mann--Whitney; $n_a=10$, $n_b=13$). The within-setup paired contrasts go in the same direction: Felt vs.\ Paste, $W=1$, $p=0.004$, $d_z=-1.95$ ($n_{\mathrm{pairs}}=10$); Gel vs.\ Paste, $W=7$, $p=0.005$, $d_z=-1.03$ ($n_{\mathrm{pairs}}=13$). The ordering Felt $<$ Gel $<$ Paste therefore holds in every contrast we can run.

\subsection{Paste-TCRE Cross-Setup Bridge}
The paste TCRE is recorded in both setups, so its between-setup behaviour acts as a sanity check on residual cross-setup variance that is not attributable to construction. Figure~\ref{fig:paste_bridge} shows the three endpoints on paste TCRE in the felt setup vs.\ the gel setup. None of the three Mann--Whitney contrasts separate the two cohorts at conventional thresholds (alpha SNR $U=89$, $p=0.15$, $d=+0.51$; reactivity $U=46$, $p=0.25$, $d=-0.39$; SSIM $U=69$, $p=0.83$, $d=+0.01$; $n_{\mathrm{felt}}=10$, $n_{\mathrm{gel}}=13$). At these sample sizes the bridge has roughly 60\% power against a Cohen's $d$ of $0.8$, so we cannot rule out smaller between-setup effects. What the bridge does rule out is a between-setup effect of the magnitude we see across constructions in §3.5---the Felt-vs-Gel SSIM contrast of $d=-2.52$ is more than three times the largest paste-bridge effect---so the cross-construction findings are unlikely to be driven primarily by acquisition-side differences between the two setups.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Methods Harmonization}
The strongest contribution of this pilot is the harmonized pipeline itself. By excluding adaptive cleaners (ICA, ASR, automated bad-channel interpolation) and keeping the only mandatory steps a notch and a linear-phase bandpass, we ensure that any between-construction differences are not artifacts of a cleaner behaving differently on clean paste vs.\ noisier felt. The cost of this choice is that subjects with bad channels stay flagged rather than rescued, which we report transparently in QC.

\subsection{Felt-tEEG vs.\ Gel-tEEG: Same Estimator, Same Geometry}
Both setups use the same hardware Laplacian on TCREs of identical ring geometry, so the cross-construction contrast in §3 is on construction (electrolyte and coupling: saline-soaked felt vs.\ conductive gel vs.\ paste) rather than on estimator.

The most striking single number in §3 is the SSIM ordering Felt $0.31 <$ Gel $0.52 <$ Paste $0.71$, with very large effect sizes on both Felt-vs-Gel and Felt-vs-Paste. The temptation is to read this as ``felt is the most aggressive spatial filter,'' but a few caveats keep us from that reading. SSIM does not separate two channels that carry genuinely independent neural information from two channels that share a common neural signal corrupted by independent noise; both produce low SSIM. The alpha-SNR ordering does not mirror the SSIM ordering either---paste is the cleanest channel by SNR, while felt and gel sit close together---which makes the ``felt-is-cleanest-Laplacian'' reading awkward at best. Several electrolyte-level effects could plausibly drive the contrast we see. Saline can spread beyond the nominal ring footprint, effectively enlarging the outer-ring contact area; the three rings can carry asymmetric impedances, which would push construction-dependent imbalances into the on-board Laplacian; and each ring contributes its own contact noise. Per-ring impedance logging in a future cohort is what would actually discriminate between these. For now we read the SSIM ordering as a hypothesis-generating observation about electrolyte coupling, not as a verdict on which construction is the better spatial filter.

\subsection{Disc-EEG Alpha SNR Exceeds tEEG Alpha SNR}
Disc EEG has the highest alpha SNR of any class in our cohort (median 4.33~dB). On its face this looks unflattering for the Laplacian, which is supposed to improve focal-source SNR by rejecting volume-conducted broadband activity~\cite{besio2006,koka2007}. The standard surface-Laplacian explanation applies here: posterior alpha during the eyes-closed condition is broadly distributed across occipital cortex rather than focal, so the same spatial high-pass that helps a focal source rejects much of a diffuse one. We report the disc--tEEG SNR contrast for context, not as evidence against the TCRE.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item Pilot-scale cohort. Felt is $n=10$ (3 long-felt, 7 short-felt) and gel is $n=13$ after QC; any single-subject outlier visibly moves the medians and inferential claims should be read as preliminary.
\item ADC clipping was prevalent on early felt sessions (impedance issues). Per-channel exclusions retain the rest of each affected subject but reduce per-class effective $n$ for some channels.
\item Paste-TCRE recordings in the two setups were collected on different days and likely by different operators. The cross-setup bridge therefore estimates an upper bound on construction effects, not a strict null.
\item VEPs are reported only for the felt setup. The gel-era VEP anomaly is not yet root-caused and is documented qualitatively in the supplement.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What This Enables}
For practitioners, this paper offers a single open pipeline and a cross-setup bridge design that other TCRE labs can re-use to make their results readable against ours. We deliberately do not recommend one construction over another on the basis of this pilot; the contribution is the comparison framework, not a winner.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusions}

We presented a pilot, methods-harmonized comparison of three TCRE constructions (saline-soaked felt, conductive gel, paste) using a single minimal preprocessing pipeline and scale-invariant endpoints. Across constructions, alpha-band detection and reactivity were physiologically plausible, the paste-TCRE cross-setup bridge produced no significant between-setup differences ($n_{\mathrm{felt}}=10$, $n_{\mathrm{gel}}=13$), and tEEG vs.\ eEEG spectrograms differed structurally and in a construction-dependent way (median SSIM Felt~$0.31 < $ Gel~$0.52 < $ Paste~$0.71$, with Felt vs Gel and Felt vs Paste both reaching $p\leq 0.002$). Conclusions are descriptive given the small and imbalanced cohort. Future work will (i)~grow both cohorts beyond pilot scale, especially balancing the long-felt arm, and (ii)~re-collect a gel-setup VEP dataset on a single amplifier under the harmonized pipeline.
